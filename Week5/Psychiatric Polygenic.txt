
Psychiatric Polygenic Risk Scores as Predictor for Attention Deficit/
Hyperactivity Disorder and Autism Spectrum Disorder in a Clinical
Child and Adolescent Sample

Arija G. Jansen1,2 · Gwen C. Dieleman2 · Philip R. Jansen1,2 · Frank C. Verhulst2,3 · Danielle Posthuma1,4 · Tinca J. C. Polderman1

In this case study, the goal was to investigate whether PRS (polygenic risk scores) for ADHD and Schizophrenia (SCZ) can distinguish ADHD and ASD (Autism Spectrum Disorder) cases from controls in the sample. The authors mentioned that from genetic studies, there was indication of a partly shared generic diathesis underlying neurodevelopmental disorders. This can mean that there is a connection between ADHD and ASD using PRS as a measure.

Exploring this insight is the motivation for this case study, along with proving a genetic distinction between PRS associated with ASD, ADHD and SCZ. Their hypothesis was then that the ADHD, ASD and SCZ PRS would be associated with the ADHD/ASD diagnosis status. 

For the collection of non-genetic information, CBCL data, a survey for emotional, behavioral, and social problems in children and adolescents, was used to collect sensitivity data to hopefully perform both a correlation and linear regression analysis. This would end up being correlation and logistic regression due to data consistency constraints. The genetic data based on the motivation of the case study was extracted from saliva, and the genotyping was performed on the Illumina PsyhChip array. It should also be noted that the procedure was approved by the ethical committee of the Erasmus Medical Center.

To properly prepare the genetic data, variants in the clinical sample were filtered based on minor allele frequency (MAF <1%), Hardy-Weinburg disequlbrium (P < 1 X 10^-6) and SNP call rate (<95%). At a higher level of specification, participants were filtered based on relatedness (pairwise Identity-By-Descent (IBD) > 0.185), genotype and phenotypic sex mismatch, outlying heterozygosity and non-European ancestry. Additionally, prior to the calculation of PRS, the SNPs were pruned to remove variants in to remove variants in LD. The polygenic scoring was performed with the software package PRSice, and the PRS for ASD, ADHD and SCZ was constructed using the most recent summary statistics at the time of the analysis. 

A psychiatric output sample called "Inside Out" was analyzed which included the CBCL data mentioned before. Using the outpatient sample and population based control sample, a correlation table was constructed to conclude that the correlation between the ADHD/ASD sample between the CBCL syndrome scale scores and the ADHD PRS P-value thresholds were too low to warrant a linear regression analysis. Therefore logistic regression was used for the analysis instead. 

With a 8 principal components (PCs) across all models and with sex as a covariate (baselinemodel), it was concluded that there was a significant  association between the ADHD PRS and the combined ADHD/ASD status, and the separate ADHD status. In contrast, the results suggested a disorder-specific effect of ADHD associated with SNPs instead of a shared common genetic mechanism with ASD. Additionally, contrary to the expectation of the authors, ASD and SCZ PRS were not associated with any of the diagnostic groups. As a final conclusion on the initial goal, it was found that the PRS of ADHD is significantly associated with the combined ADHD/ASD and ADHD status. However, the association is primarily driven by ADHD status which suggests disorder specific genetic effects of the ADHD PRS.

Reinforcement for the chosen methods of the case study was that the procedure was consisted of an interview that was part of standard clinical practice: the Clinical Interview for Children and Adolescents and the Diagnostic Interview Schedule for Children. In study, generic and clinical information was used for the children with an ADHD diagnosis, and a sample of combined ADHD and ASD diagnosis were used where comorbidity of ADHD and ASD was not allowed. These are important considerations that fill gaps in the data which help build the case for the case study's current methods.

The results also concluded that all ADHD PRS P-value threshold remained significant after Bonferroni multiple testing correction in both the ADHD/ASD and ADHD sample, but not the ASD sample. Specifically, the most lenient P-value threshold of P < 1 had a significant association in the ADHD/ASD sample. 

A P-value threshold was used to measure the accuracy of the predictive model This was an ideal choice for the case study because it aligns well with the output from logistic regression. The purpose of a P-value is to determine if the results of an experiment or study are statistically significant or simply the result of random chance. Being that this case study was based the logistical regression model this evaluation metric is an ideal choice. 

Discussion and recommendations for future similar studies revolved around a lack of available data for a prevalence rate in SCZ among the ASD population. They also mentioned that the generic overlap between ASD and SCZ and SCD and ADHD might be too small to detect the genetic overlap between the two disorders in the data. 

Some concerns the authors had was the sex distribution between the samples. Having this skew problem could potentially affect the observed associated between the PRS and diagnoses. At the end, they expressed interest in delving deeper into the association between ADHD PRS and the specific emotional and behavioural problems in larger samples (echoing the discussion). This data may provide additional information on specific problems or the severity of problems withing a diagnostic status.
